01 / The problem

Snap. Rip. Riiiiip. Crunch.

That's the sound of a helpless shark struggling to break free of binding nets tied by humans. It was just trying to get its food. Why should it be kept out of its own territory?

“Shark attacks are rare. There are better solutions to a problem like this.”

People are killing sharks because sharks can kill humans. That's not how it works. Put a big seal next to a human. There are only a few differences—and sharks don't have eyesight like us. In fact, shark attacks are rare. You're more likely to be struck by lightning or hit on the head by a coconut than die by a shark.

I do understand that others want to cull sharks, especially if they (or a relative) have been harmed by one, but there are definitely better solutions to a problem like this.

02 / The impact

Now it's not just sharks in danger.

Shark nets can catch other marine animals, too. The ocean's wildlife is connected—and the consequences can spread.

03 / The bigger picture

It can affect the whole food chain.

An expert himself said that culling sharks is not going to have any effect on shark attacks.

If too many sharks are culled, it can upset the balance between the food chain. If this keeps happening, eventually it's going to affect us and the sea animals we all love.

Protect the balance

Sharks are part of a complex ocean ecosystem. Protecting marine life means considering the wider effects of what we do.

04 / Solutions

There are things we're doing—and can do—to help.

Your text highlights conservation work, protected species, and everyday choices that can help.

Progress is possible

An organization called the Australian Marine Wildlife Conservation (or AMWC) stopped some of the Western Australia shark cull. Together, everyone has stopped both illegal shark fin fishing and most overfishing of all sharks.

Some species are now protected, including the Porbeagle, School Shark, and White Shark.

Small choices can help

  • Buy sustainable seafood. Check what you're buying.
  • Skip shark fin soup. Avoid restaurants that sell it.
  • Read labels. Check products before purchasing.
  • Look out for squalene. Avoid beauty products with squalene or shark liver oil.
05 / Take action

So, what are we waiting for?

Even other animals are being harmed from this. It's not the sharks' fault we look a bit like seals, and shark attacks are rare anyway. People are doing things to help, and you can too.
